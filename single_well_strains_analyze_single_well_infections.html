This document analyzes the single virus per well infection experiment - single_well_strains. The purpose of this analysis is a quality control check on the individual viral stocks to validate that each stock contains the expected barcode(s) and to assess the level of barcode contamination. Additionally, it provides estimates of the titer of each viral stock. These titers should be interpreted carefully as they assume each infection was performed in the linear range (where there is a linear relationship between the amount of input virus and resulting spike-in normalized counts), which may not always be true for every virus in these experiments. The plots below are interactive: mouse over for details.

Overview

Synthetic plates of one strain per well, built so that each well holds one thing the analysis has to get right.

Barcode composition of each well

The multi-panel plot below can be used to examine the barcode composition of each well and help flag any QC issues that may need further attention. Each bar is one infected well, labeled by the strain it was infected with. Hover over a bar to outline that well in all four panels.

How to read this plot

The first panel classifies every read sequenced for the well into the following categories:

  • valid barcode: the read contains a valid barcode present in either the viral library or the neutralization standard set.
  • low quality barcode: the read contains poor-quality nucleotide sequencing within the barcode region.
  • invalid outer flank: the read contains a different outer flanking sequence than specified. This outer barcode is used to separate samples on a shared sequencing run, so reads belonging to other samples on a multiplexed run are counted here and are expected in this sequencing regime.
  • invalid barcode: the read contains a barcode sequence not present in the viral library, the neutralization standard set, or the discarded barcode set.
  • discarded barcode: the read contains a barcode present in the discarded barcode set or within 1 nt of one.

The second panel takes every valid barcode read (from the "valid barcode" category in the first panel) and plots the fraction of these that map to the expected strain. This is a rough proxy for the titer of the expected virus and can help with further QC interpretation, especially for low titer strains.

The third panel classifies every barcode found in the well into the following categories:

  • expected: the barcode maps correctly to the infecting strain.
  • other library: the barcode maps to another strain in the library.
  • ≤1 nt from expected/standard: the barcode is within 1 nt of an expected barcode or a neutralization standard barcode. These are likely sequencing errors as opposed to contamination.
  • >1 nt from expected/standard: the barcode is further than 1 nt from every expected and neutralization standard barcode.

Note that this excludes neutralization standard barcodes and discarded barcodes, and values are plotted as the fraction of non-neutralization-standard reads.

The fourth panel takes every barcode in the "other library" category of the third panel and classifies it into the following categories:

  • 1 well away: the barcode belongs to a strain expected in a well one well away in any direction on the same plate, including diagonally.
  • over 2 wells away: the barcode belongs to a strain expected in a well on the same plate but over 2 wells away.
  • another plate here: the barcode belongs to a strain expected in a well on a different plate.
  • not assayed here: the barcode belongs to a strain in the library but not assayed on the plates measured here.

Empty wells are not plotted but appear in the tables below if over a threshold of 0.5% (library contamination) or 2% (unknown barcodes).

Estimated titer of each strain

These single virus per well infection experiments allow for an estimated titer of each strain. This is simply computed as the ratio of reads containing the expected barcode to the neutralization standard reads in a well, summed over all of the strain's barcodes (the number of barcodes is shown when hovering over points). The titer is relative, in arbitrary units rather than infectious units. These titers should be interpreted carefully as they assume each infection was performed in the linear range (where there is a linear relationship between the amount of input virus and resulting spike-in normalized counts), which may not always be true for every virus in these experiments. Additionally, the total counts for both the expected strain and the neutralization standard are displayed when hovering over points. These can be used to help assess the precision of these estimates.
